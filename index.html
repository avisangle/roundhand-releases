Orate

Hold a key, speak, and the text lands where you were typing. On-device transcription — audio never leaves your Mac.

Download for macOS

Requires an Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or later) and macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Intel Macs are not supported.

Installing

  • Open the downloaded .dmg and drag Orate to Applications.
  • Launch it. macOS blocks the first launch of an unsigned app; the note below is the way through.
  • Orate then asks for Microphone and Accessibility permission — it needs both to hear you and to insert text.
  • On first run it downloads the speech model (about 470 MB), once.

macOS will refuse to open Orate the first time. You get a dialog saying it cannot be checked for malicious software, offering to move it to the Trash. Don't: click Done (or Cancel on macOS 14) and leave the app where it is.

The reason is that this build is unsigned and unnotarized. Orate is not yet enrolled in Apple's paid developer programme, so macOS has nothing to verify the app against, and it warns in the strongest words it has. The warning is about the missing signature, not about anything found in the download.

To let it through, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to the security messages near the bottom, and click Open Anyway beside the one naming Orate. Authenticate, confirm once more, and Orate opens — and keeps opening from then on. This is the only route on macOS 15 Sequoia and later, where right-click → Open no longer works.

Expect one more round later. macOS marks each executable inside the app separately, and Open Anyway clears only the one you launched, so when Orate installs its first update its updater helper is blocked in the same way, for the same reason. A single command clears them all in advance. It is deliberately not printed here, because "paste this command to get past a macOS security warning" is also what fake install pages say; ask for it and it will be sent to you directly.

Microphone and Accessibility are a separate matter. Those two are macOS asking your permission rather than warning you about the app, and Orate will still ask for them once it is signed.

Updates

On its second launch Orate asks whether it may check for updates automatically. If you say yes, it checks about once a day and shows you what changed before installing anything — nothing is installed silently.

You can always check by hand from the menu-bar icon, or Settings → General → Check for Updates.

Privacy

  • Audio is transcribed on your Mac and is never uploaded or written to disk.
  • No telemetry and no accounts.
  • Optional cleanup through an LLM runs only with your own API key, and only on the transcript text.
